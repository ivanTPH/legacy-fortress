High-priority journeys, in the design language from the redesign

Cast: Ivan Yardley owns the Fortress. Bob Smith is his executor. Jacqui Yardley is family and is linked with no access. Journeys 1, 2 and 6 are shown from Bob’s or Jacqui’s side. New patterns introduced here: context banner (whose Fortress you’re in), locked row, flow stepper, step rail form, status timeline, camera overlay. Medium-priority screens follow (7–12). Placeholder data is used where the live system wasn’t visible (organisation names, admin numbers, timings).

1Linked Fortresses

The workspace switcher under the logo becomes the single place to change Fortress. When you’re inside someone else’s Fortress, a persistent ink banner says whose it is and what you can do, and every record is either permitted (normal row) or locked (lock icon, no detail, no count of what’s hidden beyond “1 record not shared”).

4Add / Edit record forms (V4 template)

This replaces the earlier stepped form. Every record type now uses one single-page form, the same as V4 4a. Required fields sit in a short block at the top, each with a “Required” tag. Edit mode uses the same template with the fields prefilled.

6Executor / death reporting

The tone is calm and plain, with no celebratory colour. Reporting a death is a deliberate secondary action, never a primary button on the overview. After submission, a status timeline shows each gate (Reported → Evidence reviewed → Death verified → Authority confirmed → Access granted). A persistent note explains that Vault access opens only at the last step.

12Alerts and exceptional states

There are three levels, each with one component. An inline banner sits at the top of the page for persistent conditions. A toast appears bottom-centre for 5 seconds to confirm a transient result. A full-page state is used when the page can’t be shown. Colour encodes severity: red for security or errors, amber for warnings or pending, neutral for info. Every alert says what happened and gives one next step.
